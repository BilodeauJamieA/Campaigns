When word spread across the kingdoms that the banners of Weeping Marrow's Glen had been raised, Norville tried to ignore it. Armies were for heroes, knights, and veterans of great wars—not frightened woodsmen with too many ghosts at their back. He had spent years convincing himself that protecting lonely roads and forgotten villages was enough.

Then the refugees began to arrive.

They carried stories unlike anything Norville had ever heard. Villages reduced to ash in a single night. Great shadows blotting out the sun. Dragons descending from the clouds to leave nothing behind but scorched earth and silence. Entire companies of soldiers were said to have vanished trying to hold mountain passes against creatures that should have existed only in legends.

Among the refugees was an elderly woman carrying nothing but a worn satchel filled with keepsakes from her family. Her children had stayed behind to delay the dragons long enough for others to escape. Looking through those simple mementos, Norville was reminded of the graves he had dug for his own companions years before. Once again, ordinary people had chosen to stand and fight while others fled to safety.

He realized that if he remained on the lonely roads, protecting one traveler or one village at a time, he would spend the rest of his life watching the world burn around him.

So he made his choice.

Norville packed his gear, fastened the old trinkets of his fallen friends to his belt, and whistled for Bert. Together they followed the streams of refugees toward Weeping Marrow's Glen, where every able soul willing to stand against the draconic onslaught was being called to serve.

He did not join seeking glory, honor, or revenge.

He joined because he had already spent one lifetime running away.

The soldiers around him came from every corner of the realm. Some were seasoned veterans, others fresh-faced volunteers, but all of them understood that there would be no safe place left if the dragons won. For the first time since Black Hollow, Norville found himself among people willing to stand their ground no matter the odds.

Fear still followed him into every drill, every patrol, and every battle. His hands still trembled before drawing his weapon, and every instinct still whispered that survival meant turning and running. But every time that voice returned, Bert stood firm beside him, and Norville remembered the promise he had made over weathered graves years before.

The man who arrived at Weeping Marrow's Glen was not fearless.

He was simply a man who had decided that, whatever happened next, he would never again let fear decide who lived and who died.
