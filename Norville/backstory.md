For as long as anyone in the village could remember, Norville had been the lanky young man with an easy smile, an endless appetite, and an uncanny talent for finding trouble. He was raised by humble herbalists on the edge of civilization where dense forests met forgotten ruins. His parents taught him how to forage, identify edible plants, and respect the creatures that lived beyond the safety of the village. They often joked that while Norville had little talent for hard labor, he possessed a remarkable gift for making friends wherever he wandered. His closest friend was a large brown dog named Bert, who seemed just as interested in finding the next meal as his master. While other children dreamed of becoming knights, famous adventurers, or powerful wizards, Norville only wished for a quiet life filled with good food, loyal friends, and enough peace to avoid anything with claws or fangs.

Life had other plans. As a young man, Norville fell in with an unusual band of companions who traveled the countryside investigating strange occurrences. Most of the time the stories of monsters, ghosts, and curses turned out to be clever tricks designed to frighten people away from hidden treasures or criminal schemes. Whenever danger appeared, Norville and Bert were usually the first to panic. They ran, hid, or found themselves accidentally creating enough chaos to distract the villains while the rest of their companions saved the day. It became an unspoken rhythm within the group. His friends never judged his fear because they knew that despite his instincts, Norville would always return when they truly needed him. After every adventure they laughed together over a hot meal, believing there would always be another village in need of help just beyond the next hill.

That illusion ended in the forgotten settlement of Black Hollow. The village had been abandoned for generations, and every tale surrounding it spoke of restless spirits and cursed ruins. The group approached it as they had every strange tale before, expecting frightened villagers, clever bandits, or an elaborate deception. Instead they uncovered something ancient that had never been meant to awaken. When the creature emerged from the darkness beneath the old crypts, Norville reacted exactly as he always had. Fear overtook reason, and he fled with Bert close at his side. Behind them echoed the sounds of steel, desperate shouts, and voices calling for one another. By the time shame overcame panic and he forced himself to return, the silence had already settled over Black Hollow.

Norville buried each of his companions himself. There was no one left to tell him the battle could not have been won or that his presence would not have changed the outcome. In his mind there was only one truth. The people who had spent years protecting him had stood their ground while he had done the only thing he had ever trusted himself to do. He had run. Standing before their graves, he swore that he would never allow fear to choose his path again, no matter the cost.

Keeping that promise demanded that he become someone entirely different. He wandered for years, seeking out hunters, rangers, soldiers, and beast wardens who could teach him the skills he had always lacked. He learned to read the wilderness, track dangerous creatures, survive with little more than a knife and a campfire, and fight with patience instead of panic. Bert trained beside him through every hardship, growing from a frightened companion into a disciplined hunting partner who could anticipate Norville's movements without a word being spoken. Together they learned that courage was not the absence of fear but the decision to keep moving despite it.

The years hardened them both. Norville's cheerful grin became a rare sight, replaced by weathered features and eyes that constantly searched every shadow. His leather armor bears the scars of battles he once would have abandoned without hesitation, and many of the wounds beneath it serve as reminders of promises kept. He still enjoys a hearty meal whenever fortune allows, and Bert has never lost his remarkable talent for sniffing out food wherever they travel. Those moments offer brief reminders of happier days, but they never last long before memories of lost friends return.

Norville does not believe he can erase the past. No amount of heroism will return those he failed, and no victory can silence the doubts that linger each time danger appears. His hands still tremble before a fight, and every instinct still tells him to turn away. The difference is that he no longer listens. Every monster he hunts, every traveler he protects, and every innocent life he saves is another step toward honoring the people who once believed he could become better than he was. He does not seek glory or forgiveness. He simply hopes that when his own journey comes to an end, he will have become the kind of man his friends always believed he could be.
