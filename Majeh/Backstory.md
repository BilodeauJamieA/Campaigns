## Overview

Majeh Remington is the most recent incarnation of myself.

I carry memories of past lives, though the earliest have long since faded into fragments. One thing, however, remains clear. The Curse. It is impossible to forget, as it is the very reason I exist in this life at all. I call it a curse for the bitterness it leaves behind: an endless cycle of lives, each one seemingly worse than the last.

I do not know how it began. Perhaps I was cursed. Perhaps I bargained with a devil. Or perhaps some god, bored and distant, chose me as a plaything.

…No. I’m rambling again. Too many memories. Too many voices.

It needs to end.

This life, at least, carries a certain irony. I was born a changeling, raised within a hidden hive between Dregmar Port and Nocturn Hollow. Like the others, I was trained until my instincts matured, until I could hold a form and craft a persona strong enough.

Shifting came naturally to me, and I never lacked for ideas. In truth, those “ideas” were not my own. They were echoes and fragments of the lives I once lived, ready-made identities waiting to be worn. At first, this was a gift. Lately, it has become… difficult to control.

Once I established my primary persona (Majeh Remington) I left the hive, as tradition demands. I entered the wider world with a singular purpose: to find a way out. Whether it be artifact, knowledge, or another dangerous bargain, I will end this cycle.

My search led me toward Caelestis. If answers exist, they will be found there.

To reach it, I joined the Resistance, working my way toward Ironreach Bastion. Once there, and after proving myself, I became an Enforcer serving as a trainer for new recruits. It is a comfortable position. My accumulated knowledge allows me to guide others effectively, while I remain careful not to stand out too much. Competent, but not remarkable. Useful, but not memorable.

It is safer that way.

I do not yet know what awaits me in Caelestis, but I am closer than I have ever been.

And this time…

I intend for it to be my last.
